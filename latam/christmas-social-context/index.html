Mood in one paragraph per country

Peru. Tired, scared, and squeezed. Peru has had four presidents in under a year (Boluarte removed Oct 2025, then Jerí, then Balcázar, then Keiko Fujimori from 28 Jul 2026), and Fujimori's honeymoon is already over: Ipsos approval fell from 57% to 42% in one month. Crime is the national obsession. Lima has been under a state of emergency again since 28 Aug, with 267 homicides in her first 50 days, extortion reports at about three an hour, and bus drivers and delivery riders shot on the job. The money pressure is new this year: Lima inflation is at an 18-year high (4.55%), led by food and fuel, and an "extraordinary" El Niño is forecast for summer 2026–27, so the heat will peak right at Christmas. Lima employment is growing, though, and unemployment is at a record low of 4.8%, so people are working but not feeling safe or better off. Peru did not play in the World Cup. Municipal elections on 4 Oct 2026 will put a new Lima mayor in place (sworn in Jan 2027) by Christmas.

Colombia. Shaken and in mourning, but cautiously hopeful. A M7.4 earthquake on 10 Aug 2026 killed about 285–288 people, mostly in Cali and Pereira, so this will be the first Christmas after a national tragedy, in two cities central to Christmas (Cali's Feria starts 25 Dec). The new right-wing president De la Espriella (since 7 Aug, won by 0.96 pts) starts with about 52% approval (AtlasIntel) and a security-first agenda. Extortion and homicides hit 10-year highs in Q1 2026, with early government claims of a fall in August. Prices are rising: inflation is 6.24% and climbing, with a year-end forecast near 7%, and consumer confidence fell 5.2 pts in August, though it is still well above 2025. Football gave a bittersweet high: Colombia went out of the World Cup unbeaten, on penalties to Switzerland in the round of 16.

Ecuador. Normalised fear and a thin wallet, but stable prices. Noboa is mid-term at roughly 44–47% approval. Homicides are slightly below 2025, the most violent year on record, but have risen again since June, with record killings of minors, a surge in El Oro/Machala, and extortion ("vacunas") now pushing even soft-drink and beer distributors out of Guayaquil neighbourhoods. Dollarised prices are flat (1.12% inflation), but only about 36.6% of workers have "adequate" jobs and informality is about 53–56%. Sectional (local) elections were moved up to 29 Nov 2026 because of the El Niño threat, so December follows straight after a campaign. Football: Ecuador beat Germany 2-1 for its World Cup moment and then went out to Mexico in the round of 32.

Strongest 10

  • Extortion is the shared nightmare of small business in all three markets. Peru has about 70 reports a day, Colombia had a 10-year Q1 high, and in Guayaquil distributors are abandoning routes. [PE][CO][EC] — Key watch-out, not a creative playground.
  • Peru: up to 6,000 bodegas could close in 2026, double the 3,000–3,500 in 2025, from extortion, gota-a-gota loans and El Niño. Women-run bodegas are hit hardest. [PE]
  • Delivery riders are dying on the job in Lima. Several riders were shot mid-delivery in 2026, one of them Venezuelan, aged 27, in Breña. [PE]
  • Colombia's 10 Aug earthquake (M7.4, about 285 dead, Cali 105, Pereira 93) makes this the first Christmas after a tragedy. It also sparked a big solidarity and collection-centre culture. [CO]
  • The Venezuelan diaspora's Christmas is complicated, not joyful. Maduro was captured (3 Jan 2026), but a double earthquake on 24 Jun 2026 killed 6,438 and damaged the Maiquetía airport. Flights from Bogotá resumed only on 1 Sep. 30% of Venezuelans in Peru consider returning within five years, and only 8% within a year. [PE][CO][EC]
  • Peru's inflation is at an 18-year high (Lima 4.55%), led by food (tomato +29%, sugar +16%) and fuel. Christmas baskets will cost more. [PE]
  • Colombia inflation is 6.24% and rising toward about 7%. The minimum-wage fight is the December story. [CO]
  • An "extraordinary" El Niño is forecast in Peru through summer 2027, with record coastal heat Sep–Nov and beyond. This means a hot Christmas and pressure on farm prices. Ecuador moved its elections because of it. [PE][EC]
  • Political fatigue: four presidents in Peru in about 10 months, Fujimori's approval down 15 pts in a month, and Colombia's narrowest runoff ever. Polarisation means politics is a no-go zone. [PE][CO]
  • Lima jobs are up 6.1% and unemployment is at a record low of 4.8%, but average income is only S/2,308 and women earn 76% of what men earn. People have work but little margin, which is the hustle that SMB delivery serves. [PE]
